# Verification scope

The writeups give the geometric arguments for Type 2 nonexistence, Type 4 open-strip coverage, the Type 5 square construction, Type 6 universality, and Type 7 rectangular coverage. Type 2 finishes with a computer-discovered resolution certificate whose individual steps can be replayed by hand. Types 4 and 6 use exact polynomial inequalities and the planar-family framework of Doyle, Lander, Schwartz, and Trettel.

The Challenge states Type 2 nonexistence and its finite sign obstruction, generic perturbation of embedded triangles, and the Type 6 flip signs and spatial lift with all twenty-four squared edge lengths matched at fixed periods. The existence statements cover the Type 4 open strip, the Type 5 unit square, all rank-two lattices for Type 6 with regularity, and all rectangular lattices for Type 7. The Solution gives the corresponding Lean proofs. [Challenge](../palomar/OrigamiTorus/Challenge.lean) · [Solution](../palomar/OrigamiTorus/Solution.lean)

For Type 2, Lean proves strict hemisphere support and a mixed-side face at each embedded flat star. A common refinement identifies the angular perimeter of the clipped enclosing triangle with that of the original link. Generic perturbation preserves the strict witnesses and embeddedness, and reflection fixes the sign of the first minor. Lean proves that the resulting signs satisfy the encoded Plücker, circuit, and mixed-side clauses. The clause generator agrees with the original 84-atom, 7,464-clause DIMACS file. The final derivation uses 741 inputs and 363 reverse-unit-propagation inferences. The Type 6 existence theorem is verified in Lean for every rank-two plane lattice. The proof uses the exact strip inequalities and a fixed-period implicit function theorem. The intrinsic development is open at vertices, along edges and inside faces, and its compact image is the entire connected quotient. The sixteen lifted areas sum to the area of one period cell. A positive-area overlap would follow from any two distinct preimages, so the development is one-to-one. The formalization transports the resulting piecewise-isometric spatial embedding through a similarity from a reduced lattice to an arbitrary lattice, using Mathlib’s modular fundamental domain. Lean verifies rank nine at the same metric-scaled spatial vertices. Conformal length variations supply the seven angle directions, period variation supplies two more, and the invertible fixed-period length residual transfers all nine to the spatial map. The dimension count modulo similarities is given in the hand writeup. For Types 4 and 7, Lean assembles the signed corner derivatives and identifies the left kernel of the horizontal cone-angle derivative with the inverse chord equations on both trapezoidal charts and the rectangular family. The assembled turning coefficient is \(-2\) times the coefficient from the negative-block entry and exit convention in the hand proof. The circle projections and remaining balance equations imply that the only left dependences are constant. The two planar chart directions give the additional rank for the marking, including at the included boundaries. In general position, each determinant sign witness excludes any common convex combination of the edge and face. Subtracting common barycentric weights and applying Carathéodory’s theorem extends this to the exact intersection rule for arbitrary faces. Lean verifies that an embedded flat Type 1 placement collapses its valence-three star to a single triangle. The three original directions have angular perimeter \(2\pi\), so the star center is strictly interior to the replacement triangle. Replacing the star preserves the spatial image, injectivity, and every remaining vertex angle and leaves exactly seven vertex labels. All pairs of these labels are edges. The existence classification then uses Schwartz’s seven-vertex obstruction and the published construction for Type 3. Those two literature results still need Lean proofs for a classification wholly in Lean. Type 4 and 7 sharp-rhombic coverage and Type 5 universality remain open. Type 6 priority remains uncertain. Doyle, Lander, Schwartz, and Trettel report a second verified universal triangulation by degree sequence \(55666677\), which is shared by Types 4 and 6. The face list in their companion Mathematica file identifies the Type 3 construction from their main theorem. [Preprint, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21909v1#S6) · [Companion face list](https://github.com/FabianLander/universal-8-vertex-origami-torus-triangulation/blob/main/UniversalityProof.m#L18-L22)

Lean proves that an affine length-preserving edge in an embedded quotient realization with compact lifted faces is strictly shorter than every alternative lift. A straight competitor meets only finitely many lattice translates of the face lifts. The face metrics bound its image length, and equality would force it onto the same spatial segment. Lean then bounds the period of a noncontractible three-edge cycle to eight vectors, with six on the sharp boundary. [ShortestWinding.lean](../OrigamiTorus/ShortestWinding.lean)

Lean also checks shortestness directly for the two Type 5 radical constructions. Strict coordinate comparisons suffice for the square. The three lattice-direction inequalities suffice uniformly for the nonrectangular box. [Type5Shortest.lean](../OrigamiTorus/Type5Shortest.lean)

The winding proof gauges the seven tree edges to zero by summing shifts along paths from vertex 0. The two basis three-cycles determine the two free periods. The face equations determine every other edge, since a third shift follows whenever two sides of a face are known. Lean checks that this propagation reaches every edge for Types 4, 5, and 7. For coherent lifted-face data giving a bijective quotient development, the translated tiles form a locally finite closed cover of the plane. Overlapping tiles share a labeled vertex lift. Connecting a tile to its primitive translates by such overlaps gives two closed edge walks, which prove that the marking is unimodular. The candidate theorem combines unimodularity with strict shortestness.
