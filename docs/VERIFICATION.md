# Verification scope

The writeups give the geometric arguments for Types 1 and 2 nonexistence, Type 4 and Type 7 open-strip coverage, the Type 5 radical constructions, and Types 3 and 6 universality. Type 2 finishes with a computer-discovered resolution certificate whose individual steps can be replayed by hand. Types 4, 6 and 7 use exact polynomial inequalities and the planar-family framework of Doyle, Lander, Schwartz, and Trettel.

The Challenge states nonexistence for Types 1 and 2, the Type 2 finite sign obstruction, generic perturbation of embedded triangles, and the Type 6 flip signs and spatial lift with all twenty-four squared edge lengths matched at fixed periods. The existence statements cover the Type 4 and 7 open strips, the Type 5 unit square and nonrectangular box, all rank-two lattices for Types 3 and 6, Type 6 regularity, and all rectangular lattices for Type 7. The Solution gives the corresponding Lean proofs. [Challenge](../palomar/OrigamiTorus/Challenge.lean) · [Solution](../palomar/OrigamiTorus/Solution.lean)

For Type 2, Lean proves strict hemisphere support and a mixed-side face at each embedded flat star. A common refinement identifies the angular perimeter of the clipped enclosing triangle with that of the original link. Generic perturbation preserves the strict witnesses and embeddedness, and reflection fixes the sign of the first minor. Lean proves that the resulting signs satisfy the encoded Plücker, circuit, and mixed-side clauses. The clause generator agrees with the original 84-atom, 7,464-clause DIMACS file. The final derivation uses 741 inputs and 363 reverse-unit-propagation inferences. The Type 6 existence theorem is verified in Lean for every rank-two plane lattice. The proof uses the exact strip inequalities and a fixed-period implicit function theorem. The intrinsic development is open at vertices, along edges and inside faces, and its compact image is the entire connected quotient. The sixteen lifted areas sum to the area of one period cell. A positive-area overlap would follow from any two distinct preimages, so the development is one-to-one. The formalization transports the resulting piecewise-isometric spatial embedding through a similarity from a reduced lattice to an arbitrary lattice, using Mathlib’s modular fundamental domain. Lean verifies rank nine at the same metric-scaled spatial vertices. Conformal length variations supply the seven angle directions, period variation supplies two more, and the invertible fixed-period length residual transfers all nine to the spatial map. The dimension count modulo similarities is given in the hand writeup. For Types 4 and 7, Lean assembles the signed corner derivatives and identifies the left kernel of the horizontal cone-angle derivative with the inverse chord equations on both trapezoidal charts and the rectangular family. The assembled turning coefficient is \(-2\) times the coefficient from the negative-block entry and exit convention in the hand proof. The circle projections and remaining balance equations imply that the only left dependences are constant. The two planar chart directions give the additional rank for the marking, including at the included boundaries. Lean proves Type 1 nonexistence. Collapsing the valence-three star preserves the embedded image and the remaining cone angles. Flatness forces strict supports at the seven remaining vertices. Their determinant signs satisfy 2,100 circuit and Plücker clauses with a checked 418-step refutation. This proves the seven-vertex obstruction needed for the collapsed Type 1 face list. Lean proves Theorem 1.1 of Doyle, Lander, Schwartz, and Trettel for the original Type 3 face list. The proof checks the two published height vectors, inflates at fixed periods with diagonal 12, and uses the quotient homeomorphism to obtain a facewise isometric embedding for every rank-two lattice. Lean proves that an affine length-preserving edge in an embedded quotient realization with compact lifted faces is strictly shorter than every alternative lift. A straight competitor meets only finitely many lattice translates of the face lifts. The face metrics bound its image length, and equality would force it onto the same spatial segment. Lean then bounds the period of a noncontractible three-edge cycle to eight vectors, with six on the sharp boundary. [Winding/ShortestWinding.lean](../OrigamiTorus/Winding/ShortestWinding.lean)

Lean also checks shortestness directly for the two Type 5 radical constructions. Strict coordinate comparisons suffice for the square. The three lattice-direction inequalities suffice uniformly for the nonrectangular box. [Type5/Shortest.lean](../OrigamiTorus/Type5/Shortest.lean)

The winding proof gauges the seven tree edges to zero by summing shifts along paths from vertex 0. The two basis three-cycles determine the two free periods. The face equations determine every other edge, since a third shift follows whenever two sides of a face are known. Lean checks that this propagation reaches every edge for Types 4, 5, and 7. For coherent lifted-face data giving a bijective quotient development, the translated tiles form a locally finite closed cover of the plane. Overlapping tiles share a labeled vertex lift. Connecting a tile to its primitive translates by such overlaps gives two closed edge walks, which prove that the marking is unimodular. The candidate theorem combines unimodularity with strict shortestness.
