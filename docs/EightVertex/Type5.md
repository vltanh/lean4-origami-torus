# Type 5 embeddings of flat tori

Type 5 has a piecewise-linear isometric embedding for every nonhexagonal flat torus, at every scale. Exact Python certificates cover the reduced moduli with $0\le x<1/2$ and the open ray $x=1/2$, $y>\sqrt3/2$. The square and a closed near-square box also have Lean proofs. [Off-ray proof](Type5FoldedModuli.md) · [Open-ray proof](Type5SharpEmbeddings.md) · [Lean box theorem](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/ContinuationTorus.lean)

Universality remains open at the hexagonal modulus $(1+i\sqrt3)/2$. Every embedding there must have face 125 on its convex hull. This reduces existence to twelve explicit polynomial feasibility tests. Exact hexagonal folds realize eleven of the twelve clique sign classes, and each recorded example has crossings. The regular planar lifting construction also fails for all 62 archived folding patterns. [Hexagonal feasibility tests](Type5HexagonalAngles.md) · [Exact examples](Type5HingeChart.md) · [Planar lifting obstruction](Type5PlanarMasks.md)

Separate radical families give a uniform strip around the rectangular ray, two closed oblique boxes, and a sector along the equal-length arc. Lean verifies a planar facewise isometry along the entire sharp ray. Any embedding on that ray admits a perturbation at fixed modulus with a rational core and all 70 spatial determinants nonzero. [Rectangular and oblique tori](Type5Rectangles.md) · [Equal-length arc](Type5Rhombic.md) · [Sharp-ray folds](Type5FoldedModuli.md) · [Fixed-period perturbation](Type5Regularity.md)

On each sharp torus one can prescribe any sufficiently small positive shortening of the squared length of edge 01 while matching the other 23 lengths in an embedded Type 5 placement. The limiting length vector has positive distance from those of exact flat embedded Type 5 tori, even with varying periods. [Construction and exclusion proof](Type5Opening.md)

## The coordinates

Use the [oriented Type 5 faces](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Reconstruction.lean) in Lutz numbering and the [integral edge shifts](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Square.lean) for the lattice generated by \((1,0),(0,1)\). The shifts sum to zero around every face and the shifts from vertex 1 vanish. The following is an exact construction at this marking.

The neighbors common to all three leaves \(0,6,7\) are the anchors \(1,2,5\) and the roots \(3,4\). Use vertex 1 as the origin. In a square of side 64, take the following grid points with a common displacement \(\delta=64s^2\), where \(s=1/512\). Divide both coordinates by 64 to return to the unit square. [Dyadic-grid construction, PR #3](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-origami-torus/blob/fe5a6eee0cf14dc8cfb0614505d63812f1bbfa25/research/type5_completion/dyadic_scale.md)

| Vertex | First coordinate | Second coordinate |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2 | \(29\) | \(-32+\delta\) |
| 3 | \(32-\delta\) | \(5\) |
| 4 | \(-4\) | \(27\) |
| 5 | \(-2\) | \(-12\) |

Take the three extrinsic anchors in the horizontal plane at their intrinsic coordinates. For a vertex \(j\) being reconstructed, write \(u=p_j\) and \(v=(v_x,v_y,v_z)\). If \(i\) is a core neighbor, put \(s_i=p_i-K_{ij}\), where \(K_{ij}\) is its oriented shift toward \(j\). The squared distance to the origin must agree, so \(\lVert v\rVert^2=\lVert u\rVert^2\). Subtract this equation from the squared distance equation for \(i\) to get

\[
r_i\cdot v-s_i\cdot u=(\lVert r_i\rVert^2-\lVert s_i\rVert^2)/2.
\]

For anchors 2 and 5 these equations determine \((v_x,v_y)\) by a two-by-two solve. A root’s intrinsic coordinates are already fixed, so its height is the signed square root of \(\lVert u\rVert^2-v_x^2-v_y^2\). For this witness use the positive sign at vertex 3 and the negative sign at vertex 4.

For a leaf, the horizontal solution is \(B+Lu\), with \(B\) a vector and \(L\) a two-by-two matrix. Substituting this in the equations from roots 3 and 4 gives \(z_i v_z=a_i\cdot u+b_i\) for \(i=3,4\). Eliminating the height gives the equation \(n\cdot u+m=0\), where \(n=z_4a_3-z_3a_4\) and \(m=z_4b_3-z_3b_4\). Write

\[
u(t)=-\frac{m}{\lVert n\rVert^2}n+t(-n_y,n_x),\qquad
v(t)=\left(B+Lu(t),\frac{a_3\cdot u(t)+b_3}{z_3}\right).
\]

The polynomial \(\lVert v(t)\rVert^2-\lVert u(t)\rVert^2\) is quadratic for each leaf in this witness. Use the positive square-root sign for leaves 0 and 7 and the negative sign for leaf 6. The formula satisfies each leaf’s four linear equations and the remaining norm equation, hence all five squared distances to its neighbors. The graph has no edges joining the leaves and no edge 34, so this accounts for all 24 distances. The [Lean witness](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Square.lean) verifies the equations, the necessary nonzero denominators and all five radicands. It checks the strict inequalities on the exact radicals by bounding them between rational endpoints using the order properties of squaring and division.

## The geometric conclusion

Place the intrinsic triangles using the vertex coordinates and the indicated shifts, then reduce modulo \(\mathbb Z^2\). The cocycle identities and skew symmetry make the two lifts of every shared edge agree up to a translation, so these maps join continuously. Each oriented affine face map has positive determinant. This makes the map locally open in an edge’s interior, since its neighboring face images lie on opposite sides. At a vertex the consecutive oriented sectors sweep positive angles less than \(\pi\) and their total is a positive multiple of \(2\pi\). Thus the map is locally open there as well.

The sum of signed double areas is exactly 2, so the oriented map to the square torus has degree one, the lattice having covolume one. Each value off the images of edges has one preimage, since all local signs on face interiors are positive. If a value had two preimages, choose disjoint neighborhoods of those points. Local openness makes their images overlap in an open set containing a value off the image of the edges. This would give two preimages there. The map is onto, since its image is open and compact in the connected target. It is therefore a homeomorphism and the assigned intrinsic triangles triangulate the square torus.

The spatial coordinates are in general position. The determinant sign witnesses exclude common points of each disjoint edge and face. To see that this suffices, suppose two faces have a common point with barycentric coefficients that disagree on their shared labels. Subtract common mass on the shared labels, then divide the two remaining sums by their common total. This gives equal convex combinations with disjoint supports of size at most three each. If their union has size six, there is a nonzero linear change of the six coefficients preserving the two separate sums and the three coordinate equations. Starting with positive coefficients, move along that change until one reaches zero. Thus an intersection beyond the intended shared face gives equal convex combinations on at most five distinct points with positive weights. General position requires at least five points in an affine dependence. The only possible support sizes are then two and three, which is an excluded disjoint edge–face intersection. The same argument handles unintended intersections involving edges and vertices, since these are contained in faces. The resulting piecewise-affine map into \(\mathbb R^3\) is an embedding. Each corresponding pair of triangles is congruent by the three squared edge lengths, so their gluing gives the desired intrinsic metric.

Lean proves the full square realization in `OrigamiTorus.Type5.square_torus`. It verifies the cocycle, directed side pairings, every face orientation, total area, and the spatial simplicial embedding. The intrinsic development is a homeomorphism to the square lattice quotient, and the spatial embedding preserves the distance between any two points of each lifted face. Lean proves multiplicity one by translating the lifted triangles into a fundamental cell. Their areas sum to the area of the cell, and local openness would force a positive-area overlap if two points mapped to the same value. [Type5/Periodic.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Periodic.lean) · [Lattice/PeriodicArea.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Lattice/PeriodicArea.lean) · [Geometry/SpatialRealizations.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Geometry/SpatialRealizations.lean)

Every marked square edge is strictly shorter than all alternative lifts. The same coordinate interval checks prove both unit-direction inequalities for all 24 edges. ## The near-square box

Take the lattice generated by \((1,0)\) and \((x,y)\), with
\(0\le x\le 2^{-36}\) and \(1\le y\le 1+2^{-36}\).
Use the same integral shifts and radical signs as above.
Interpret an integral shift \((m,n)\) as the physical vector \((m+nx,ny)\).
Take \(s=1/512\) and \(X=29/64\). The physical core points are

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_2&=\left(X,\frac{(s^2-\tfrac12)(x^2+y^2)-xX}{y}\right),&
p_3&=\left(\tfrac12-s^2,\tfrac5{64}y\right),\\
p_4&=\left(-\tfrac4{64},\tfrac{27}{64}y\right),&
p_5&=\left(-\tfrac2{64},-\tfrac{12}{64}y\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Setting \(x=0\) and \(y=1\) recovers the square core. The
interval calculation covers the whole box and certifies the two root
radicands, the three leaf normals, the leading coefficients and
discriminants, and all sixteen intrinsic face orientations. The affine
determinants and disjoint edge–face tests then give the spatial embedding
throughout the box, using the same general-position argument as for the
square. [Type5/Continuation.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Continuation.lean) ·
[Type5/ContinuationCertificate.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/ContinuationCertificate.lean)

The linear map \((a,b)\mapsto(a+xb,yb)\) converts lattice coordinates to
physical coordinates and multiplies signed areas by \(y\). In lattice
coordinates, the full signed double area is 2 for arbitrary vertex
representatives with this marking. Each contribution depending on a vertex
position cancels when the shared sides are paired. The full physical double
area is therefore exactly \(2y\), and the same multiplicity-one argument
applies to the lattice of covolume \(y\). The resulting Type 5 embedding realizes
the intrinsic torus of modulus \(x+iy\) for every point of the closed box.

The three strict directional checks also imply shortestness against every
nonzero period. For a reduced basis \(a,b\), every lattice vector is a
nonnegative integer combination of one of the adjacent pairs among
\(\pm a,\pm b,\pm(b-a)\), whose mutual dot product is nonnegative. A
nonnegative integer is bounded by its square, so the strict inequalities
for the two directions give the strict inequality for the combination.

## The eight-parameter reduction

Fix a set of oriented edge shifts that is skew-symmetric and sums to zero
on each face. The data of a marked realization consist of an embedded
flat spatial placement, intrinsic vertex representatives, and matching
lengths on each nondegenerate lifted face. Changing intrinsic representatives
by the shifts from vertex 1 makes those shifts zero. Translating the
representatives gives $p_1=0$. The face $152$ has zero shifts on all three
edges. Its three lengths let us move the spatial anchor triangle by a
rigid motion to $0,(p_2,0),(p_5,0)$. Lean constructs the linear isometry on
the anchor plane by the two congruent side vectors and extends it to
space. [Type5/Normalize.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Normalize.lean)

The four intrinsic core points $p_2,p_3,p_4,p_5$ supply eight real
parameters. The roots have at most two signed heights each. For each such
choice, the three leaves have independent linear systems because no two
leaves are adjacent. A nonzero eliminated normal gives an affine line, and
the nonzero norm polynomial on that line gives at most two candidates,
including the degree-one case. Each embedded flat placement with the
prescribed marked lifts appears on the resulting list of at most 32. The
list may contain repeated candidates and candidates that fail the remaining
realization tests. [Type5/MarkedReduction.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/MarkedReduction.lean)

### Geometric exclusions

The core spans space. To see this, suppose the five core points are planar.
Each root lies outside the filled anchor face by injectivity. Express each
in affine coordinates with respect to the anchors. A nonzero affine
relation with at most two labels on each sign side forces disjoint vertices
or edges to intersect, provided the two roots do not have the same strict
sign. Give the first root coefficient 1 and the second coefficient 0.
The first root lies outside the anchor triangle, so at least one anchor
coefficient in the relation is positive. The two positive entries and
the zero root coefficient rule out three negative entries. There must
therefore be three positive entries.
The corresponding relation with coefficients 0 and $-1$ has three
negative entries. Add a positive multiple of the second relation to the
first. The root coefficients have opposite signs throughout, so three
positive entries or three negative entries must occur at every positive
multiple. These are disjoint open conditions, and both occur, contradicting
connectedness of the positive real line. [Geometry/PlanarCompleteGraph.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Geometry/PlanarCompleteGraph.lean) ·
[Type5/Geometry.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Geometry.lean)

Opposite root rays in a flat leaf or anchor star split its link into two
paths. Their angle lengths are each at least $\pi$ and add to $2\pi$,
so both are minimizing semicircles. The checked link order supplies two
interior neighbors on one path, with the requisite edges to the two roots.
The angular triangle equality puts each of their rays in the positive cone
of the other ray and one endpoint. If the disjoint edges do not intersect,
the two cone coefficient sums are both less than one. Substitution then
makes one interior ray proportional to an endpoint ray, contradicting
injectivity along the radial edges. Any collinear consecutive triple of
leaf neighbors would have its middle point between the other two, because
otherwise the two link edges would overlap. The outer vertices must be
the nonadjacent roots. The middle anchor would have opposite root rays,
which is excluded by the same argument. [Geometry/EmbeddedEdges.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Geometry/EmbeddedEdges.lean) ·
[Type5/Geometry.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Geometry.lean)

A flat embedded leaf also has no common nonzero direction in all its face
planes. If adjacent planes differ, their shared ray must be proportional
to any direction common to both. At a leaf the only possible opposite
neighbor pair is the root pair, already excluded, and two distinct
neighbors cannot have the same ray. Hence there is at most one change of
plane around the cycle. One change cannot occur in a cycle, and no changes
would put the entire core in a plane. [Type5/LeafPlanes.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/LeafPlanes.lean)

### Removing the two algebraic degeneracies

Suppose the two independent intrinsic coordinate directions could both be
matched by spatial variations in the four subtracted length equations.
Then the five pairs of relative neighbor vectors satisfy $S_i=LR_i+b$
for a linear map $L$. Put $V_i=(R_i,S_i)$ and give their ambient space
the bilinear form $B((R,S),(R',S'))=R\cdot R'-S\cdot S'$. The five
vectors span at most four dimensions, and their Gram matrix vanishes on
the diagonal and on each entry for consecutive vertices. Its determinant
is twice the product of the five complementary entries, one for each
diagonal of the pentagon. Since it vanishes, three consecutive vectors
are mutually orthogonal. The projection onto the intrinsic coordinates
is injective on their span, because the restricted quadratic form
vanishes and a spatial vector with squared norm zero is zero. Thus the
three vectors are linearly dependent. When $b\ne0$, the dependence
has coefficient sum zero and gives a collinear triple of spatial neighbors,
which was excluded above.

When $b=0$, the symmetric bilinear form
$B_L(R,R')=R\cdot R'-(LR)\cdot(LR')$ vanishes on the first
two-dimensional face plane. Choose a vector transverse to this plane.
The linear functional obtained by pairing with that vector has a
nonzero kernel vector $e$ in the plane. This $e$ pairs to zero with
the plane and the transverse vector, hence with all of space. Taking
$d=Le$ gives equal norms and equal pairings with every corresponding
neighbor. On each face the intrinsic-to-spatial isometry sends $d$ to
the orthogonal projection of $e$ to that face plane. The equality of
norms forces $e$ to lie in every face plane, a contradiction. Therefore
the eliminated linear equation has a nonzero normal.

If the norm polynomial vanished identically along a solution line, the
values at $0,1,-1$ relative to the actual leaf would give a nonzero
intrinsic direction and a corresponding spatial direction with equal norms.
The two nonzero parameter values give the matching inner product with
the anchor ray. The homogeneous length equations give the matching inner
products with all five rays. The same projection argument again puts the
spatial direction in every face plane. This proves the norm polynomial
is nonzero. [Type5/LeafRank.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/LeafRank.lean) ·
[Type5/Nondegenerate.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Nondegenerate.lean) ·

## A hexagonal fold with two edge–face crossings

At \(\tau=1/2+i\sqrt3/2\), there is a Type 5 map from the hexagonal torus into \(\mathbb R^3\) which is affine and isometric on all sixteen faces. In physical coordinates, with the square marking, take
\[
p_1=0,\qquad
(p_2,p_3,p_4,p_5)=
\frac1{1024}\big((56,-252),(494,130),(-185,307),(-409,-284)\big).
\]
Both roots have positive height. In the quadratic formula for leaves 0, 6 and 7, take the signs \(-,+,+\). The five radical choices give all 24 edge lengths, and every intrinsic face has positive oriented area. The degree-one development therefore identifies the domain with the hexagonal torus. The Lean theorem gives the map on the complex lattice quotient and proves the distance identity for every pair of points on a face. [HexagonalFold.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/HexagonalFold.lean) · [Reconstruction for arbitrary core points and branches](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/RadicalChart.lean)

Exactly two of the 138 disjoint edge–face pairs intersect. Edge 13 meets triangle 024, and edge 02 meets triangle 136. For each pair, put the edge labels first, followed by the three face labels. The five alternating affine determinants have signs \((-,-,+,+,+)\). Their affine dependence gives equal convex combinations with all coefficients strictly positive, placing each crossing in the relative interiors. The other 136 pairs have disjoint closed convex hulls. The distance-rigidity theorem also rules out repairing this fold by moving its spatial vertices while retaining the 22 determining edge lengths. Any embedded realization at this modulus must change at least one of those lengths. A different core gives exact embeddings on the open sharp ray. Only the hexagonal similarity class remains open. ## Restrictions on a realization at an arbitrary modulus

Every vertex of an embedded flat Type 5 placement admits a plane through it with all its neighbors strictly on one side. The flat-link argument gives such a plane once antipodal neighbor directions and a planar star have been excluded. The cyclic link checks exclude antipodal pairs. A planar star would put five pairwise adjacent vertices in a plane, contradicting embeddedness of their edges. Consequently every four pairwise adjacent vertices span a tetrahedron of nonzero volume. A coplanar Radon partition would put one vertex in the convex hull of three neighbors or make two disjoint edges meet. [Type5/Geometry.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Geometry.lean) · [Type5/StrictSupport.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/StrictSupport.lean)

For any such placement, 22 of its 24 edge lengths determine all 28 pairwise distances. Omit edges 46 and 47. Distances to the tetrahedron with labels 1, 2, 5, 3 determine all distances among the seven vertices other than 4. The tetrahedron with labels 1, 2, 5, 0 then determines the distances to vertex 4. The conclusion holds even if the comparison placement lies in a higher-dimensional real inner product space. [Type5/DistanceRigidity.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/DistanceRigidity.lean)

The unweighted harmonic placement used for the intrinsic hexagonal triangulation has no spatial realization for **any** positive-definite period metric. To see the obstruction, take the six cliques with labels \(1,2,5,r,j\), where \(r\in\{3,4\}\) and \(j\in\{0,6,7\}\). In each clique the distances determine the Gram matrix of the four vectors from vertex 1. All six determinants must vanish in \(\mathbb R^3\). Normalize the period Gram matrix to have entries \(1,t,h\), so \(h-t^2>0\). For the harmonic displacements, an exact polynomial identity expresses a positive constant times \((h-t^2)^3\) as a linear combination of the six determinants with quadratic polynomial coefficients. This excludes the prescribed lengths even for self-intersecting placements. The intrinsic vertex positions can vary at fixed modulus, so the hexagonal case and Type 5 universality remain open. [Type5/HarmonicObstruction.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/HarmonicObstruction.lean)

The obstruction is uniform as the lattice varies. Normalize the two period vectors so that their largest absolute Cartesian component is one. There is a single \(\varepsilon>0\) such that for every spatial placement, the sum over the 24 edges of the squared differences between spatial and harmonic squared lengths is at least \(\varepsilon\). To see what happens at a rank-one limit, write the period form as \((u\,dx+v\,dy)^2\). The anchors 1, 2, 5 then lie on a line. Projecting the distance equations for leaves 6 and 7 onto that line gives \((u-v)^2(u+v)=0\) and \(u^2(u-2v)=0\). Their only common solution is \(u=v=0\). Compactness after translation and rescaling gives the positive lower bound. The proof does not compute its numerical value. [Type5/HarmonicGap.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/HarmonicGap.lean)

## Symmetry of an embedded realization

Every embedded flat Type 5 realization has a labeled edge-length assignment with trivial stabilizer in the automorphism group of the triangulation. The following geometric proof uses the preceding distance-rigidity theorem. The symmetry conclusion is a geometric argument outside the Lean formalization.

The vertices of degree seven are the three anchors 1, 2 and 5. The second face on each edge of triangle 125 singles out a different leaf. An automorphism fixing the anchors therefore fixes all three leaves, then both roots by the face list. The permutations \(s=(1\;2)(3\;4)(6\;7)\) and \(r=(0\;6\;7)(1\;2\;5)\) preserve the face list and generate all permutations of the anchors. It suffices to treat these two conjugacy classes. [Labeled face list](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/Reconstruction.lean)

If an automorphism preserves edge lengths, the rigidity theorem applied to the relabeled placement gives equality of every pairwise distance. Four affinely independent clique vertices determine a unique ambient Euclidean isometry implementing the permutation. Its order equals the order of the permutation, and affinity identifies its fixed points on the surface with those of the simplicial action. [Distance rigidity](../../OrigamiTorus/EightVertex/Type5/DistanceRigidity.lean)

For \(s\), the fixed points on the surface are edge 05 and the segments from its endpoints to the midpoint of edge 12 in faces 012 and 125. Their spatial images form a simple closed triangle. Its corners are noncollinear since 0, 1, 2 and 5 form a nondegenerate tetrahedron. The nonidentity rigid motion fixes these three points, so it reflects in their plane. Vertices 1 and 2 lie on opposite sides. The edge path \(1,3,2\) must meet the plane, although it avoids the entire fixed set on the abstract torus. This contradicts injectivity.

For \(r\), the ambient motion is a rotation of order three. Its axis meets the embedded surface at exactly three points, the two fixed vertices 3 and 4 and the barycenter of face 125. Around each point, a sufficiently small centered sphere meets the surface in an invariant simple closed curve made of great-circle arcs. The two axis poles avoid this curve because the fixed points are isolated. The two closed complementary disks on the sphere are both invariant under the rotation. By the Brouwer fixed-point theorem each contains a pole, so the axis crosses from one side of the surface to the other at that point. The two sides of a closed connected embedded polyhedral surface in three-space belong to the bounded and unbounded complementary components. Both ends of the axis are in the unbounded component, which requires an even number of crossings. Three is impossible.

This restriction applies at every modulus. Existence of an asymmetric embedding at the hexagon remains open. In the explicit hexagonal fold above the squared anchor lengths \(12,15,25\), multiplied by \(1024^2\), are \(66640,247937,217249\). Even at this modulus the facewise isometry equations admit a scalene anchor triangle.
