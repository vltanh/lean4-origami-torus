# Arbitrary Type 4 embeddings are covered by the eight full domains

This note assembles the research-level coverage implication at the single
target

    tau*=1/2+10^6 i,    y=10^6,    W=10^12,    epsilon=1/1000.

**Coverage theorem.** If the flat torus of modulus `tau*` has an embedded,
facewise-isometric Type 4 realization, then, after relabeling by a Type 4
automorphism, an isometry of the same lattice, vertex-lift gauge changes,
and ambient translation, its data satisfy one of the eight full domains
`ray1_1,...,ray1_6,ray2_1,ray2_2` generated by
`type4_fixed_cubic.py --full`. They also satisfy the corresponding
affine-anchor domain generated by `direct_metric.py`.

The implication includes arbitrarily small cap edges and clique volumes,
nonclique coplanarities, and all broadened-strip endpoints. None of the
normalizations changes the target modulus.

The proof below makes Gate 0 complete as a mathematical reduction, using
the existing finite marking/orbit theorems and the exact cell enumeration.
It is **not a newly assembled Lean theorem**, and it excludes none of the
eight continuous domains. The remaining formal packaging is listed in
section 8.

## 1. Extract the lifted intrinsic data

Use the standard Type 4 oriented face list

    012 031 024 053 045 152 134 146 165 264 257 276 374 356 367 475.

It agrees with `Type7.type4Faces` in
[Rectangular.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Type7/Rectangular.lean).

Start from a facewise-isometric embedding of the actual flat quotient
`R^2/Lambda`, where `Lambda=Z(1,0)+Z(1/2,y)`, with this triangulation.
Equivalently, start from the labeled flat spatial triangulation together
with its intrinsic isometry to that quotient. If the latter is specified
only up to a common scale, rescale the spatial realization to this lattice.

Choose one lift `p_i` of every intrinsic vertex. Each closed intrinsic
face is a disk and therefore lifts through the covering `R^2 -> R^2/Lambda`.
Its lift is a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle: the intrinsic face is
isometric to its nondegenerate spatial triangle, and an isometry between
connected planar triangle interiors is a rigid planar map. The lift
extends to the boundary. Two face lifts along a common edge differ by a
constant lattice translation, because they lift the same edge path.

Thus there are antisymmetric integer shifts `K_ij=(m_ij,n_ij)` such that
every lifted edge displacement is

    p_j-p_i+(m_ij+n_ij/2, y*n_ij),

and their sum around each oriented face is zero. Every face has one common
orientation sign, since the intrinsic identification is a homeomorphism
of oriented surfaces. The physical signed double areas sum to `+2y` or
`-2y`: face interiors partition one torus of area `y`. In normalized
coordinates `p_i=(x_i,y*b_i)`, the corresponding sum is `+2` or `-2`.

This supplies the coherent lifted-face data, degree one, and quotient
bijection used as inputs by the existing winding theorems. These are
consequences of the given realization, not additional geometric hypotheses.

## 2. Every edge is the unique shortest lift

The spatial map is globally distance nonincreasing relative to the
intrinsic metric. Subdivide an intrinsic path at its finitely many face
crossings and use the face isometries; a straight lifted competitor is
covered by finitely many translated compact face triangles. Approximation
or subdivision at edge/vertex contacts gives the same length bound.

For an actual edge with endpoints `i,j`, let `ell` be its intrinsic length.
Its spatial image is a Euclidean segment of the same length. Therefore

    ell=|r_j-r_i| <= dist_T(i,j) <= ell.

If another lattice lift had this same shortest length, its straight
intrinsic path would have image length at most `ell`, joining points at
Euclidean distance `ell`. Equality forces that image to traverse the same
straight segment. Injectivity makes the two intrinsic paths equal, contrary
to their different lifted displacement. Every alternative lift is therefore
strictly longer. This is also the conclusion of
`strictShortest_of_lattice_face_cover` in
[ShortestWinding.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Winding/ShortestWinding.lean).

In physical coordinates every edge displacement `(X,Y)` consequently
satisfies

    |X|<1/2,
    |Y/y + X/(2W)|<1/2+1/(8W),
    |Y/y - X/(2W)|<1/2+1/(8W).

In particular its normalized vertical displacement has absolute value
less than `1/2+1/(8W)`. Every nonedge spatial chord is at most the distance
of **every** intrinsic lattice competitor, so all nine competitor bounds
used by the finite formulas are necessary.

## 3. Exactly two marked cocycles suffice

The quotient identification gives a unimodular marking. One way to see
this directly is to lift the entire triangulation: its translated faces
tile the plane. Edge walks connect a face to each of its two primitive
lattice translates. Thus the image of integral cycle periods contains
both primitive generators. The finite face-propagation identity expresses
every such period in the two basis periods of cycles `014` and `015`, so
the matrix of those two periods has determinant `+1` or `-1`.

The corresponding formal endpoint is
`marking_unimodular_of_quotient` in
[WindingGeneration.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Winding/WindingGeneration.lean).
It requires coherent lifted faces and a bijective quotient development;
section 1 supplies exactly these inputs.

Summing the strict edge bounds around any noncontractible triangular cycle
places its period in

    {(1,0),(-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(1,-1),(-1,1)}.

The arithmetic bound is `triangle_mem_allowed`. The theorem
`marking_mem_candidates_of_shortest_edges` in
[WindingMarkings.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Winding/WindingMarkings.lean)
then places the marking in the twelve Type 4 sharp-ray candidates, with
no separate assumption on primitive triangle periods.

The finite `orbit_partition` / `exists_orbit_representative` theorems in
[WindingOrbits.lean](../../OrigamiTorus/Winding/WindingOrbits.lean) reduce
these twelve candidates to

    ray 1: ((-1,0),(-1,1)),
    ray 2: ((-1,1),(0,1)).

The right actions are lattice isometries at this same `y`; the left
actions are actual automorphisms of the Type 4 faces. Orientations may
reverse, which is why the domain permits either common face-area sign.

Now gauge at the universal vertex 4. If the current cochain is `K`, replace
`p_i` by `p_i+K_4i` in lattice coordinates, and replace the cochain by

    K'_ij=K_ij+K_4i-K_4j.

All shifts out of 4 become zero. Translate intrinsically and spatially
so `p4=r4=0`. The face cocycle equations and the two basis periods determine
the remaining shifts, so the cochains are exactly `SH[0]` and `SH[1]` of
[type4_gap_enum.py](../type4_focus/type4_gap_enum.py). Substituting the
two displayed matrices into `Triangulation.coefficients .type4` and
performing this gauge reproduces both archived rows exactly.

Since the radial edges `4i` are shortest,

    |x_i|<1/2,    |b_i|<1/2+1/(8W).

## 4. Finite two-gap localization applies to this realization

Sections 1--2 of the
[finite localization proof](../type5_focus/fixed_elongated/README.md)
use only eight vertices, sixteen faces, and strict shortest lifts. Their
argument therefore applies to the Type 4 realization without any
additional Type 5 premise. Here are the quantitative steps needed for
the coverage implication.

For the reference horizontal fibers put `F(s)=f([(0,ys)])`. Each fiber has
length 1 and extrinsic diameter at most `1/2`. If `v_f` is the unit
vertical direction of a lifted face, then on its vertical interval

    |F(t)-F(s)-y*(t-s)*v_f| <= 3/2.

Trim `epsilon` from each endpoint of the sixteen projected face intervals.
Overlapping trimmed intervals give vertical-direction error at most
`3/(2 epsilon y)`. Within one connected union component the error is at
most `beta=45/(2 epsilon y)<=9/400`. The complement consists of gaps, all
within `epsilon` of one of the eight vertex levels, and has total length
at most `16 epsilon`.

Subdivide a period into at most 34 pieces at interval endpoints. The fiber
estimate, the gap length, and `|F(s+1)-F(s)|<=1/2` give

    |sum_C length(C)*v_C|
      <=16 epsilon+beta+(51+1/2)/y <1/25.

Thus each union component has length less than `13/25`.

At a gap, every point of its horizontal fiber has positive barycentric
weight on vertices within circular distance `epsilon` of the gap.
Otherwise a face containing that point would have its trimmed interval
covering the gap. Normalize those near-vertex weights and set the others
to zero. This is a continuous simplexwise homotopy into the induced
near-vertex subcomplex, including at edge and vertex contacts. The
horizontal homology is preserved. An edge-walk decomposition therefore
gives an essential simple cycle in this subcomplex.

Edges between near vertices cannot change the coherent vertical lift by
an integer: their vertical displacement would be at least `1-2 epsilon`,
contradicting section 2. The essential cycle is horizontal and primitive,
and uses at least three vertices.

Three gaps pairwise more than `2 epsilon` apart would require nine
vertices. All gaps cannot lie within `2 epsilon` of a single gap, because
that would leave a union component longer than `13/25`. Consequently
two gap centers `s,t` cover all gaps by their radius-`2 epsilon`
neighborhoods. The component-length bound gives

    119/250 < t-s < 131/250.

Using the common vertical direction on the intervening long component
gives `|F(t)-F(s)|/y>9/20`. Each epsilon-near vertex cluster lies in a
ball of radius `epsilon*y+1/2` about its reference point. Their convex
hulls therefore have separation greater than `11y/25`.

These estimates are at the fixed finite target. They use no limiting
cap area, no asymptotic subsequence, and no separation of degeneration
scales. A chord in a near cluster again has zero coherent vertical shift.
Splitting the essential cycle along chords therefore leaves an essential
chordless horizontal cycle in each cap.

## 5. The exact closed-cell cover retains all endpoints

Name the two caps so vertex 4 lies in the first cap or the following open
strip. Assign labels `0,1,2,3` to first cap, first open strip, second cap,
second open strip. Choose integer lifts `q_i` with `q4=0` so
`a_i=b_i-q_i` is in one coherent period interval. The radial bounds and
the center separation give `q_i in {-1,0,1}`.

Collapse each epsilon cap monotonically to a turn. An edge cannot strictly
cross a gap with both endpoints outside its epsilon neighborhood: any
incident face would put that gap inside a trimmed face interval. Its
collapsed interval therefore lies in a closed half-turn. This condition
depends only on the labels and integer lift differences, so the
quarter-unit test in `type4_gap_enum.py` is exhaustive. Its cap-cycle
test is justified by section 4, not imposed as a new conjecture.

The exact enumeration yields 56/9 relative cells for the two markings.
Pass to maximal cells by allowing a strip label to absorb either adjacent
cap label, with the checked integer lift change at the seam. In the actual
finite geometry this means using the **closed broadened strips**

    [s-epsilon,t+epsilon],    [t-epsilon,s+1+epsilon].

Every original cap neighborhood absorbed into a strip is contained in
the corresponding broadened interval. A cap label retained by a maximal
cell remains a cap label from the original cell. Hence the cap diameter
and separation bounds remain valid for its designated cap vertices.
There are 10/4 maximal cells.

The checked marking stabilizers act by actual lattice isometries,
relabelings, and integer gauges. They preserve epsilon neighborhoods,
the broadened intervals, and all metric conditions. The quarter-unit
action records the same integer gauges and possible interchange of the
two cap names. Their orbits give exactly the following eight domains.

For each center, nonemptiness of its lower/upper interval is checked by

is retained where the maximal cover requires a weak endpoint.

## 6. Every remaining assertion is necessary

The points of this normalized realization satisfy all recorded metric
and geometric conditions:

* **Face metric and degree:** section 1 gives all 24 edge equations,
  coherent strict areas, and signed total double area `+2` or `-2`.
* **Nearest lifts and contractions:** section 2 gives the strict edge
  comparisons and all required nonedge upper bounds. A nonzero marked
  edge shift has strictly positive unshifted metric slack.
* **Cap distances:** points of one retained cap have squared distance at
  most `4 epsilon^2 W+1/4`, using the same coherent vertical lift and a
  horizontal competitor of displacement at most `1/2`. Opposite-cap
  squared distances are strictly greater than `W*(11/25)^2` by section 4.
* **Strict clique metrics and support:** the direct proof in
  [global_attack_support.md](global_attack_support.md) derives all 23
  strict K4s and strict neighbor support from the Type 4 embedded flat
  stars. It uses no vertex deletion or seven-vertex nonexistence premise.
* **Ray-2 extras:** the four-edge winding-cycle proof in
  [ray2_exact_phase.md](../type4_focus/ray2_exact_phase.md) gives the global
  phase and tube estimates. The polarization calculation in
  [fixed_metric.md](../type4_focus/fixed_metric.md#2-exact-product-bounds-for-the-entire-second-marking)
  gives the three product inequalities. Both apply to the entire second
  marking before choosing a cap cell.
* **Embedding circuits:** an intersection of disjoint labeled simplices
  is impossible for the given embedding. Thus every complete affine-circuit
  exclusion holds, including lower-support circuits and nonclique zeros.
  The direct formulation's strict-support cuts are necessary by the same
  support theorem. If mixed-face cuts are included, their strict opposite
  signs follow from projecting a supported flat spherical link: a polygon
  whose every edge supports all its vertices would be convex in an open
  hemisphere, with perimeter strictly less than `2*pi`.

For the older signed-Gram formula, form the actual Euclidean Gram matrix
and the four missing slacks. Its pivot has inertia `(2,2)` because
`S12,S36>0`; the remaining Schur complement is positive semidefinite of
rank one, so its five required square-root coordinates exist. This
supplies all auxiliary unknowns.

For the direct formulation, the strict anchor `(4,5,1,6)` is an affine
basis. Express the four other spatial vertices in that basis. Its Gram
matrix is positive definite and determined by its six prescribed edges;
the other eighteen edge equations hold. The four derived nonedge
distances are the actual chords. All cubic bracket conditions therefore
hold, including those with zero nonclique brackets.

This completes the implication to one of the eight full domains.

## 8. What remains

At the research-proof level, the reduction gate now has a complete
implication from an arbitrary realization to the finite full-domain
cover. **All eight domain exclusions remain open.** A solver timeout is
not an exclusion, and a local core certificate does not replace a full
row proof.

For a kernel-checked final theorem, still formalize the extraction of
coherent lifted faces from the chosen realization interface; the Type 4
strict-support specialization; the quantitative finite two-gap argument;
the closed-cell transport and center elimination; and the final assembly
with whichever row certificates are eventually obtained. The existing
shortestness, candidate-marking, and orbit theorems can then be reused
with the inputs explicitly supplied here. None of these unassembled
formal steps is being reported as already Lean-verified.
