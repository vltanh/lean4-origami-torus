## 1. A uniform quantitative two-turn theorem

Let f be any embedded flat realization with the Type 5 triangulation and sharp lattice generated by (1,0),(1/2,y), y>=10^6. The only geometric facts used in this section are eight vertices, sixteen faces, and strict shortest edge lifts (the latter follows directly from injectivity and straight edge images). Set epsilon=1/1000, and let b denote the normalized vertical circle coordinate.

Let F(s)=f((0,ys) mod Lambda), s real. Then F is y-Lipschitz, and ||F(s+1)-F(s)||<=1/2 by the shear and the horizontal closed geodesic of length one. Every point on the horizontal fiber of level s is within extrinsic distance 1/2 of F(s). In any lifted face f0, write u_f0,v_f0 for its two orthonormal horizontal and vertical image directions. The horizontal span of the face is less than 1/2 by the strict nearest-lift inequalities. Thus for any s,t in its vertical interval,

    ||F(t)-F(s)-y*(t-s)*v_f0|| <= 3/2.                       (1)

This follows by choosing points in the face at the two levels: their horizontal difference is at most 1/2, and each is within 1/2 of the corresponding F value.

The normalized vertical span of every face is <1/2+1/(8*y^2), since every side obeys |b|<1/2+1/(8*y^2). Trim epsilon off each endpoint of each of the sixteen lifted vertical intervals, discard empty intervals, and project the resulting open intervals to the vertical circle. Let U be their union and call the points of its complement gaps. The gaps are within epsilon of some vertex level, because the untrimmed face intervals cover the circle. Hence the measure of the gaps is at most 16*epsilon.

If two trimmed intervals overlap, choose compatible lifts of the intervals; the untrimmed intervals overlap in a segment of length at least 2*epsilon. Subtract (1) for the two faces across this segment:

    ||v_f0-v_g0|| <= 3/(2*epsilon*y).

Within any component C of U the overlapping-interval graph is connected and has at most sixteen vertices. Choose a reference unit direction w_C. For every face contributing to this component,

    ||v_f0-w_C|| <= beta := 45/(2*epsilon*y) <= 9/400.

Split a period of the circle at the trimmed endpoints and its two endpoints. There are at most 34 pieces; each piece in U belongs to some trimmed interval, using continuity at endpoints. On it use (1) and the direction comparison. On gap pieces use the y-Lipschitz bound. Summing increments over the period and using the endpoint mismatch yields

    ||sum_C length(C)*w_C|| <= 16*epsilon + beta + (51+1/2)/y < 1/25.

The reverse triangle inequality now gives length(C)<(1+1/25)/2=13/25 for every C. In particular U cannot be the entire circle.

## 2. Every gap requires a short essential cycle

At a gap s, consider its actual intrinsic horizontal geodesic (a simple essential circle). In every simplex it meets, each point of that geodesic has positive barycentric weight on at least one vertex within circular vertical distance epsilon of s. Otherwise the minimum and maximum vertical levels among its positive-weight vertices would lie strictly more than epsilon below and above s in a compatible lift. Then the face's trimmed interval would contain s, a contradiction. This reasoning also covers the case where s is itself a vertex level, or a point lies on an edge.

Normalize the weights on the near vertices to sum one and set the other weights to zero. The resulting map of the geodesic into the induced subcomplex on the near vertices is continuous (on simplex overlaps the formula agrees); the simplexwise straight homotopy preserves its horizontal homology. Approximate the loop by an edge walk and decompose it into simple cycles. At least one of these cycles is essential. Every edge between near vertices has vertical lift within the same epsilon neighborhood, since a winding to another copy would require a vertical displacement >=1-2*epsilon, violating the nearest-lift bound. Thus these cycles are horizontal; an essential simple cycle is primitive and has at least three distinct vertices. In particular at least three vertices lie within epsilon of every gap.

No three gaps can have pairwise circular distances >2*epsilon, as the three disjoint near-vertex sets would require nine vertices. Nor can all gaps lie within 2*epsilon of one chosen gap: then U would contain an arc of length >=1-4*epsilon>13/25. Consequently there exist two gaps s,t at circular distance >2*epsilon, and all gaps lie in their two radius-2*epsilon neighborhoods. Let d in (0,1) be the oriented distance from s to t. The two open arcs between these neighborhoods belong to U. If d<=4*epsilon or 1-d<=4*epsilon, the other arc has length >=1-8*epsilon>13/25, impossible. Their lengths d-4*epsilon and 1-d-4*epsilon are both <13/25, so

    119/250 < d < 131/250.                                   (2)

For the arc from s+2*epsilon to t-2*epsilon, its length is >119/250-4*epsilon. Sum (1) along its subdivision into at most 34 pieces, use the common direction and the Lipschitz bound on the two deleted endpoint segments. This yields

    ||F(t)-F(s)||/y >= (d-4*epsilon)*(1-beta)-4*epsilon-51/y
                     > 9/20.

Each near-vertex cluster lies in a closed extrinsic ball of radius epsilon*y+1/2 centered at its reference F value. Its convex hull does also. Therefore the two cluster convex hulls have distance strictly greater than

    (9/20-2*epsilon)*y-1 > (11/25)*y.                         (3)

Each has at least three vertices and contains a primitive horizontal cycle. This is a uniform theorem at positive finite y; it does not assume convergence, transverse cap area, or a regular asymptotic expansion. In particular it covers all previously unhandled endpoints of the maximal limit families.

## 3. Exact finite branch classification

Gauge the archived unique Type 5 sharp marking so every edge shift out of vertex 1 is zero. Let b_i be the actual lifted normalized vertical coordinate (b_1=0). Rotate the vertical coordinate so the two gap centers are at s and t=s+d, choosing their names so vertex 1 belongs to the first cap or first open strip.

Use labels 0,1,2,3: the first epsilon cap, open interval between caps, second epsilon cap, remaining open interval. Choose actual representatives a_i=b_i-q_i in [s-epsilon,s+1-epsilon), with q_1=0 by a common change of the representative of s. Endpoints at the second cap are included in that cap; the right endpoint of the interval is excluded. Since vertex 1 is universal, |b_i|<1/2+1/(8*y^2), so q_i in {-1,0,1}.

There is a continuous monotone degree-one map of the lifted vertical line collapsing both epsilon neighborhoods to turns 0 and 1/2 and linearly rescaling the intervening arcs. For any edge its lifted interval cannot strictly cross either gap with both endpoints outside that gap's epsilon neighborhood, because then that gap would belong to the corresponding trimmed face interval. After the monotone collapse, each edge is consequently contained in some closed half-turn interval [k/2,(k+1)/2]. An edge between vertices near the same cap cannot wrap to the next copy of that cap: its original length in normalized vertical coordinate would be >=1-2*epsilon. These observations include edges between the two caps (which may take either half-turn).

It follows that the **same finite combinatorial enumeration** used previously for the limiting problem is now exhaustive at the finite metric. Reproduce it independently in generate.py. Representative values 0,1/4,1/2,3/4 suffice for this label test because being contained in a closed half-turn depends only on the labels and the integer lift difference, not the exact interior positions. Require at least three vertices at each cap, vertex 1 in labels 0 or 1, and all edge intervals contained as above. There are 36 raw (label,integer-q) pairs. The checked marking symmetry (6,5,2,4,3,1,0,7), inducing physical vertical reflection (m,n)->(m+n,-n), is valid at all sharp moduli. Together with renaming the gap centers it yields 19 representatives (two fixed cells and seventeen pairs).

These are continuous branch systems, not 19 discrete realizations. They cover every actual embedding, including cap collinearity, additional vertices at a turn, and all equalities in the near-cap bounds. The symmetry acts on the actual representatives and near-cap neighborhoods because reflection preserves epsilon and swaps the strip lengths; no asymptotic assumption is used.

## 4. Linear bounds without turn-level unknowns

Write a_i=b_i-q_i. The exact gap localization implies the following constraints for some s,t satisfying (2):

* label 0: s-epsilon <= a_i <= s+epsilon;
* label 1: s+epsilon < a_i < t-epsilon;
* label 2: t-epsilon <= a_i <= t+epsilon;
* label 3: t+epsilon < a_i < s+1-epsilon.

Each condition is a lower or upper bound on one center, and both centers have nonempty lists of lower and upper bounds. Eliminate them exactly: every lower bound must be <= every upper bound on the same center, using < if either bound is strict. Also require every lower bound on s plus 119/250 to be < every upper bound on t, and every lower bound on t to be < every upper bound on s plus 131/250. These are necessary and sufficient for the existence of the two centers with the strict separation interval, since the possible t-s form an interval whose endpoints are the extreme lower-minus-upper and upper-minus-lower differences. This yields a finite list of linear inequalities on the original 14 intrinsic coordinates alone.

Add two metric consequences. Normalize all squared distances by y^2. For two vertices in the same cap, the normalized squared distance is <=4*epsilon^2+1/(4*y^2): use their intrinsic vertical difference <=2*epsilon and a horizontal representative with displacement <=1/2, then metric contraction. For every opposite-cap pair, (3) gives normalized squared distance >(11/25)^2. These tests apply to the four nonedges as well as edges.

## 5. Exact 18-variable fixed-metric systems

Fix y=10^6 (w=10^12). Use intrinsic positions (x_i,b_i), i!=1, where x is physical horizontal lift and b is normalized vertical lift, and four additional real variables for normalized squared distances of the missing edges. Edge squared distances are (X^2/w)+B^2, with X=x_j-x_i+K_ij^m+K_ij^n/2 and B=b_j-b_i+K_ij^n. Impose the strict nearest-lift inequalities |X|<1/2 and |B+/- X/(2*w)|<1/2+1/(8*w); nonedge contraction against nine lattice shifts; all sixteen strict negative intrinsic face areas and signed total double area -2. Impose the linear localization and both cap distance bounds above.

The centered seven-point Gram matrix is G_ij=(d_1i+d_1j-d_ij)/2. The earlier K4 lemma guarantees positive definiteness of the (2,5,3) block A. Sylvester inequalities on A and ten bordered 4-by-4 minors give PSD rank exactly three, by Schur complementation. All 23 clique tetrahedral Gram determinants are required positive. The default necessary system omits the general embedding tests. The optional --full system adds degeneracy-complete intersection exclusions in the augmented affine Gram H=1*1^T+G_ext, where G_ext has a zero row and column for vertex 1. For each disjoint-simplex sign partition of a set S of two to five vertices (first vertex in the positive part), forbid det H_S=0 and the strict specified signs of its first-row adjugate entries. A minimal conformal affine dependence has exactly these signs and rank |S|-1; PSD ensures the first diagonal cofactor is positive. Conversely the cofactor vector certifies an intersection when these tests hold. This handles nonclique coplanarities as well as generic cases. With consistently oriented intrinsic faces of degree -1, the intrinsic PL map is a homeomorphism by the earlier local degree argument. Therefore the full system is exact for the localized problem under the inherited K4 and marking premises; the added quantitative conditions do not exclude any actual embedding. No orientation sign archive is needed.
