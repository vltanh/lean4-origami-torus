# Type 4 sharp-ray marking 2: exact phase and tube bounds

## Status

This is a quantitative necessary theorem for the **entire second sharp-rhombic marking**, not merely a relative-interior cap limit. It uses the verified winding/shortest-edge premises and elementary geometry. No asymptotic genericity or nonvanishing limiting cap area is assumed. It does not prove nonuniversality or exclude either marking. The goal is still to settle Type 4, by one impossible modulus in both markings or a construction on the whole sharp ray.

## 1. A four-edge vertical winding cycle

Write the lattice as generated by h=(1,0), g=(1/2,y), with y>=sqrt(3)/2. Use the established ray_2 marking and gauge K_4j=0, with intrinsic p_4=0. Physical intrinsic coordinates are used (not normalized vertical coordinates).

The horizontal triangular cycles 541 and 546 both have period h. Each horizontal edge displacement has absolute value strictly less than 1/2 by uniqueness of the shortest edge lift. Since three such displacements sum to 1, all three are strictly positive. Hence

    p_5=(-s,T/y),        0<s<1/2,
    1/2-s < p_1.x,p_6.x < 1/2.

The oriented cycle 4253 has period 2g-h=(0,2y); its directed shifts in this gauge are 0,g,g-h,0. Write

    p_2=(c,y/2+u/y),     p_3=(d,-y/2+v/y).

These are definitions of u,v, not assumptions about cap placement. Its four directed physical horizontal displacements and vertical corrections (relative to y/2, multiplied by y) are

    X = (c, 1/2-s-c, d+s-1/2, -d),
    A = (u, T-u, v-T, -v).

In particular sum A_i=0. Comparing each edge vector (X_i,Y_i) with the two alternate translates by g and g-h gives the strict shortest-lift inequality

    y Y_i + |X_i|/2 < y^2/2+1/8.

Define the four positive slacks

    delta_i=1/8-A_i-|X_i|/2 > 0.

They satisfy the exact transport identities and bounds

    sum delta_i = 1/2 - (sum |X_i|)/2 <= s,
    |T| < s/2,
    -3/8 < u < 1/8,
    -1/8 < v < 3/8.

Indeed each consecutive pair of horizontal displacements X_1,X_2 and X_3,X_4 has absolute sum at least 1/2-s. Summing their two strict inequalities gives T<s/2 and -T<s/2, respectively. The individual inequalities then give u<1/8, u>T-1/8>-3/8, v>-1/8, v<T+1/8<3/8. In fact each A_i belongs to (-3/8,1/8). These bounds hold at every sharp modulus, regardless of actual 3D geometry.

Consequently the intrinsic slope of edge 45 obeys the **relative** bound

    |p_5.y-p_4.y| < s/(2y).

The slacks vanish at least as fast as s in every sequence with s tending to zero. This handles the collapsing edge 45 without dividing by an uncontrolled lower bound for its length.

## 2. Explicit global tube, with no limit assumptions

Assume y>=1. Let r_i denote the extrinsic vertices and L=|r_2-r_4|. Since the edge is isometric,

    L^2=c^2+(y/2+u/y)^2.

Here |c|<1/2, and the bounds on u give

    y/2-3/(8y) < L < y/2+1/y.

For the upper bound, u+u^2/y^2 is increasing on (-3/8,1/8) at y>=1, so L^2-y^2/4<25/64, and divide by L+y/2>=y/2. The lower bound follows directly from its positive vertical component.

A translate of p_3 by g, followed by a horizontal lattice translate, differs from p_2 by vertical coordinate (v-u)/y and horizontal coordinate of absolute value <=1/2. The embedding is globally distance nonincreasing relative to the intrinsic torus metric. Thus

    |r_2-r_3| <= sqrt(1/4+(v-u)^2/y^2) < sqrt(13)/4 < 1.

The two actual edges r_4r_2 and r_4r_3 have Hausdorff distance at most |r_2-r_3|, by linear interpolation from their common endpoint. In the vertical circle R/(y Z), their intrinsic lifts cover the intervals from 0 to y/2+u/y and from -y/2+v/y to 0. The only possibly uncovered interval has length <3/(4y). A horizontal fiber has intrinsic length 1 and hence extrinsic diameter <=1/2. Every point at a covered level is therefore at distance <3/2 from edge r_4r_2. A point in the uncovered gap can move intrinsically by vertical displacement <3/(4y) and horizontal displacement <=1/2 to vertex 2, so its extrinsic distance to edge r_4r_2 is <sqrt(13)/4. We conclude:

**For every y>=1, every embedded Type 4 realization in ray_2 lies in the open radius-3/2 neighborhood of its edge 42, whose length differs from y/2 by less than 1/y.**

This gives explicit constants for the entire second marking, not just subsequential boundedness. It is compatible with both cap families from elongated_hand.md, including all their degenerations. The cycle 4253 is the reason the second marking admits this sharper argument.

## 3. Relative extrinsic tilt of the possibly collapsing cap edge

Write D_ij=|r_j-r_i|^2. Let e=(r_2-r_4)/L. Put B=|X_1|+|X_2|. From the previous inequalities,

    1/2-s <= B < 1/2+s.

Since A_1+A_2=T, subtraction of the two edge metric equations gives the exact identity

    D_42-D_25 =
      (|X_1|-|X_2|)(B-1/2)
      + delta_2-delta_1
      + T(A_1-A_2)/y^2.

Using |X_i|<1/2, |A_1-A_2|<1/2, |T|<s/2 and sum delta_i<=s,

    |D_42-D_25| < s(3/2+1/(4y^2)).

Also D_45=s^2+T^2/y^2 < s(1/2+1/(8y^2)). Polarization now gives

    |(r_5-r_4) dot e|
       = |D_45+D_42-D_25|/(2L)
       < s(2+3/(8y^2))/(2L).

For y>=2 we have 2L>13y/16, hence

    |(r_5-r_4) dot e| < 3s/y <= 3|r_5-r_4|/y.

In particular the **direction** of edge 45 is asymptotically transverse at rate O(1/y), uniformly even if s tends to zero much faster than any power of 1/y. The transverse edge length is nonzero for y>3, and its ratio to the full edge length tends to one uniformly. This removes a specific small-edge tilt loophole for a second blow-up on this marking.

## 4. What this proves and does not prove

The bounds are valid throughout ray_2, not only for either chosen cap pair, and require no additional orientation enumeration. The exact finite metric attack can use these phase variables, the radius-3/2 tube, and the relative tilt inequality directly. They do not by themselves contradict embeddedness or rule out a collinear cap. The first marking has no comparable four-edge cycle with vertical winding two, so a separate argument is needed there. The Type 4 universality question remains open.

A numerical continuation from the previously certified interior planar family was also attempted (not used in the proofs above). After recovering its intrinsic marking and scale from the universal vertex's developed star, fixed-modulus nonlinear least squares was applied to edge-length residuals with penalties for all strict determinant, face-area and nearest-lift margins. For y=1, a sequence starting at x=.49 reached x=.498 with max squared-edge residual 7.36e-9 and positive minimum numerical margin 4.40e-8 (using five times the signed volume determinants). At x=.5 the residual was 6.89e-8 and the minimum margin was -1.32e-8. This is a numerical failure to obtain an endpoint, not a certified obstruction. The square witness was also continued over part of the interior. No strict sharp-ray witness was obtained. The earlier Type 4 near-universality construction's marking is compatible with the first sharp-ray orbit; the degeneration of that particular family at x=.5 is not a marking-based impossibility.

The hand-derived identities in sections 1-3 do not depend on these numerical tests. No independent audit of this new proof is claimed, and no new closed case is recorded.
