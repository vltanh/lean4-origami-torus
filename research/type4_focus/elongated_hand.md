# Type 4 — hand proof of the elongated cap classification and bounded tube theorem

Status: Type 4 nonuniversality is still open. This note attacks the entire elongated sharp-rhombic ray, not another H13 subcase. All deductions below are by hand; no solver, numerical search, automated enumeration, or computer-assisted polynomial certificate is used. The two-marking classification from the earlier notes is a premise for the Type 4 tables. The general compactness theorem in sections 1–2 does not require that premise.

## 1. Exactly two turns at the macroscopic scale

Let a sequence of embedded, piecewise-affine isometric flat tori with eight vertices have lattices generated by h=(1,0), g=(1/2,y), with y tending to infinity. Pass to a subsequence with a fixed labeled triangulation. Write f for the embedding. Each edge is an intrinsically unique shortest path: its image is a Euclidean segment, and a second equally short intrinsic path would have the same image, contradicting injectivity. Thus for its physical intrinsic displacement (X,Y),

    |X| < 1/2,  |Y|/y < 1/2 + 1/(8 y^2).

The second bound follows by comparing the length against the two lattice translates with horizontal components +/-1/2 and vertical component of the same sign as Y, which gives y|Y|+|X|/2 < y^2/2+1/8.

Fix a spanning tree and choose lifts with its edge shifts zero. The lifted horizontal coordinates and normalized vertical coordinates are bounded by telescoping the edge inequalities along at most seven tree edges. For every remaining edge, its vertical integral shift and its physical horizontal shift m+n/2 are then bounded, so its two integer shifts have finitely many possibilities. Pass to a fixed shift cocycle. This elementary compactness reduction is enough for the general argument; no precomputed list of markings is needed.

A horizontal fiber is a closed intrinsic geodesic of length 1. Its extrinsic diameter is at most 1/2, by considering the two arcs between any pair of points. The curve

    c_y(s) = ( f([(0,ys)]) - f([(0,0)]) ) / y

is 1-Lipschitz and uniformly bounded on compact parameter intervals. Its endpoint mismatch over one period is at most 1/(2y), due to the horizontal shear. Pass to a locally uniformly convergent subsequence. On each labeled face, the unit physical horizontal and vertical directions of its affine isometry also converge along a subsequence.

On any face lift, its horizontal span is at most 1/2. If two levels in its vertical span are separated by y*(s'-s), their images, after division by y, differ by (s'-s) times the face's vertical unit direction, up to a term of norm at most 1/(2y). Replacing the chosen points by points on the reference vertical curve adds at most 1/y, because points in the same horizontal fiber have diameter at most 1/2. Every open gap between the limiting vertex levels on the vertical circle is covered by some limiting face span (the finitely many face lifts cover the torus). Hence the limiting periodic curve c is affine with unit speed on each gap. No limiting face span can have a genuine turn of c strictly in its interior.

**Turn-cycle lemma.** At every genuine turn there is a simple essential horizontal cycle in the induced subcomplex on the vertices whose limiting vertical level is that turn.

Proof: choose a level just above the turn, between it and the next distinct limiting vertex level. For all sufficiently large y, the actual horizontal fiber at that level meets only faces having at least one vertex at the turn in the limit. Otherwise a limiting face span would cross the turn, or its entire span would lie away from the chosen gap. On each intersected face retract the barycentric weights to the turn vertices by setting the other weights to zero and normalizing. The retained weight sum is positive, since all other vertices of the face are above the chosen level. These maps agree on shared faces and the straight barycentric interpolation is a homotopy of the original fiber into the turn subcomplex. The original fiber has homology h, so the subcomplex carries nonzero horizontal homology. By cellular approximation, take an edge walk representing that class and split it into simple cycles. At least one is essential. All edges joining turn vertices have zero vertical displacement in the limiting local lifts: it would otherwise be an integral nonzero vertical period, contrary to the shortest-edge bound. Thus the essential simple cycle is horizontal, and its period is +/-h by primitivity of a simple essential loop on a torus. It has at least three vertices. QED.

Distinct turn levels have disjoint vertex sets, so there are at most floor(8/3)=2 turns. A periodic, nonconstant, unit-speed polygon has at least two turns. It follows that there are exactly two, their directions are opposite, and the two parameter intervals have length 1/2. Up to rigid motion and translation of the parameter, c is a segment of length 1/2 traversed twice.

## 2. Removing the unbounded-cap-width loophole by a second scale

The preceding statement alone only bounds cap vertex heights by o(y). A cap cluster could have physical vertical width tending to infinity. We now show that this cannot prevent finding bounded horizontal cycles.

Consider either of the two turn clusters of section 1. It contains between three and five vertices, all with limiting level equal to the turn. Use coherent physical vertical lifts near that level. Pass to a subsequence on which the cluster's physical vertical width W is bounded or tends to infinity. In the bounded case keep the cluster and its horizontal cycle. In the unbounded case W/y tends to zero. Rescale the local vertical coordinate by W, center it at the cluster's lowest vertex, and rescale ambient coordinates by W about the reference fiber. The locally uniformly convergent limiting reference curve on R is again 1-Lipschitz and affine with unit speed on every gap between finite limiting vertex levels, by the same face-span argument (the fiber diameter error is now O(1/W)). Cluster levels lie in [0,1] with minimum 0 and maximum 1; all outside vertices have lifts escaping to +/-infinity locally.

At the previous scale there is a face spanning the gap immediately to the left of the old turn, whose upper limiting endpoint is that turn. Its lower endpoint is a distance much larger than W from the cluster. The new curve therefore has, on its left tail, exactly the previous left vertical direction. The corresponding face on the right gives the previous right direction. These directions are opposite. The new curve must consequently have at least one genuine turn in [0,1].

The turn-cycle lemma applies unchanged on this local scale: choose an actual horizontal fiber just above the finite turn level, and retract its barycentric weights to the vertices at the turn. Faces crossing the turn are impossible by the local face-span identity; infinite limiting endpoints cause no problem, since all other vertices of a face meeting the chosen level are strictly above it. Each genuine turn requires at least three distinct cluster vertices. Since there are at most five, there is exactly one local turn. Its cluster is a strict subset of the preceding cluster (the old width scale has distinct minimum and maximum). The two tail directions are still opposite.

If the new cluster has unbounded physical width, repeat this argument with that width. At each repetition the number of vertices strictly decreases but remains at least three. At most two such repetitions are possible. Thus, after taking a subsequence, the final turn cluster has bounded physical vertical width. All vertices excluded in the process are at physical vertical distance tending to infinity from it. The final cluster still contains an essential horizontal cycle. Perform the same procedure at the other macroscopic turn, passing to a further subsequence.

**Bounded-cap theorem.** Along every elongated sequence of eight-vertex origami tori there is a subsequence with two vertex-disjoint essential horizontal cycles near opposite macroscopic turns, each of bounded *physical* vertical width. The vertices in the two cycles have mutually separated vertical levels by y/2+o(y) on the vertical circle. All edges of either cycle have bounded extrinsic length: their physical horizontal displacements have absolute value <1/2 and their vertical displacements are the bounded differences of the coherent local lifts. This conclusion covers nested degenerations at both turns.

An induced chord of a cycle in such a bounded cluster also uses the local zero-vertical-period lift for sufficiently large y. Splitting along chords therefore yields a chordless essential horizontal cycle in the same cluster. This observation will be used below.

### Isolated cap cycles

If a final bounded cluster consists exactly of the vertices of a chordless essential cycle, every edge of that cycle has one incident face extending to each side of the turn on the last unbounded scale. Indeed the retractions of the actual fibers just above and below the turn both have nonzero homology in the simple cycle, so both images traverse each edge; there are just two incident faces. Their limiting unit vertical directions are opposite, say e and -e.

For a cap edge with intrinsic displacement (X,Y), its two face representations give exactly

    X (u_f-u_g) + Y (v_f-v_g) = 0,

where u and v are the unit horizontal and vertical directions. Dotting with e, the u terms tend to zero by orthogonality, the v coefficient tends to +/-2, and |X|<1/2. Hence Y tends to zero. The extrinsic axial displacement of the edge also tends to zero.

Orient the cap cycle to have period h. The retraction of the upper horizontal fiber, oriented along h, traverses each cap edge exactly once in this direction: each upper incident face contributes one segment joining the two sides from its apex to the endpoints of the cap edge, and there is only one such face per cap edge. To see that the oriented horizontal displacement X of each cap edge has nonnegative limit, use the intrinsic lift a,b,c of this upper face, with a,b the cap endpoints. The horizontal order of the intersections of an upper level with ac and bc is the sign of

    (x_b-x_a)(Y_c-Y_a) - (x_c-x_a)(Y_b-Y_a).

The first vertical factor tends to positive infinity, the last vertical factor tends to zero, and the horizontal factors are bounded. Since the order is the positive fiber order, a negative limiting X is impossible. Their signed sum is exactly 1 by the period. The cap cycle therefore has perimeter tending to 1, with all its vertices asymptotically in one intrinsic horizontal fiber and one extrinsic plane perpendicular to e. The limiting transverse polygon may still be degenerate. No positive lower bound on its area or on its shortest edge is asserted.

## 4. Vertical level families, and a cross-edge in each strip

For the chosen cap-pair representatives, vertex 4 belongs to the first cap A. Set its intrinsic normalized vertical coordinate to zero, retaining gauge K_4j=0. At the macroscopic limit, all A vertices have lift b=0, and each B vertex has lift b=+/-1/2. Write B+ and B- for the corresponding vertex sets. The signs are forced by the zero vertical periods of cap edges and |b_j-b_i+K_ij^n|<=1/2 on all edges. For example in ray 1 with B=256, its shift 25 forces b5=b2-1, and edge26 forces b6=b2; hence b2=b6=1/2 and b5=-1/2. In ray 2 with B=0263, edges02 and26 give b0=b2=b6, edge36 gives b3=b6-1. In each row the remaining intervals follow from the same edge inequalities.

In the last two columns the directed edges are from A to B and have limiting *intrinsic vertical displacement* +1/2 and -1/2, respectively. The sign is not necessarily the sign of the endpoint's lift, since an edge can carry a lattice shift.

## 5. Uniform bounded-tube theorem for both Type 4 markings

Take any elongated sequence of Type 4 sharp-ray embeddings, pass to one of the two fixed markings, and choose the bounded cap cycles as in sections 2–4. Their extrinsic diameters are bounded (telescope the bounded cycle-edge lengths). Let D be the physical vertical separation of representative cap levels on the positive arc, so D/y tends to 1/2. The table gives a direct cross-edge from A to B through each strip. Their physical vertical displacements are D+O(1) and D-y+O(1), since the cap widths are bounded. Their horizontal displacements have absolute value <1/2. Consequently their extrinsic lengths are D+O(1) and y-D+O(1). These two lengths differ by a bounded amount, by the Euclidean triangle inequality and bounded cap diameters. It follows that

    D = y/2 + O(1),

and both cross-edges have length y/2+O(1).

Fix one of these edges as a reference segment. The endpoints of the other edge lie within bounded distance of the corresponding endpoints of the reference segment. Convex interpolation along the two straight segments shows that their Hausdorff distance is bounded. Each physical vertical level in the interior of either strip except possibly bounded intervals near the cap levels meets the corresponding cross-edge. The point of the edge at that level lies in the same horizontal fiber as every surface point at that level, so the latter are at extrinsic distance at most 1/2 from that edge. The uncovered level intervals are within bounded intrinsic distance of a cap vertex (move vertically and then along a horizontal fiber by at most 1/2). Thus the *entire* embedded surface is at bounded distance from the reference segment. The segment is itself an edge of the surface.

This was shown after subsequence extraction for an arbitrary elongated sequence. It implies the uniform assertion: there are constants C and Y0 such that for every y>=Y0 and every embedded Type 4 realization of the sharp-rhombic modulus 1/2+iy, two cap cycles have physical vertical widths and extrinsic diameters at most C, their level separation differs from y/2 by at most C, and an actual edge of length within C of y/2 has the whole surface in its C-neighborhood. Otherwise take a sequence y>=n of counterexamples to the assertion with C=n; the subsequential bounded construction contradicts that sequence. The assertion is vacuous if no such embeddings exist beyond some threshold. It gives no explicit numerical C or Y0.

This is a global compactness result for both markings, including all intermediate-scale collapses of the other one or two vertices. It is stronger than a compactification restricted to relative interiors of fixed macroscopic cells. It does **not** prove nonuniversality: bounded transverse thickness, and even an asymptotically horizontal cap cycle, are compatible with degenerating embedded surfaces.

## 6. Remaining hand-proof gate

To settle Type 4 negatively it suffices to exclude all eight cap-pair classes in the bounded-tube regime. The new theorem removes the possibility of escaping every bounded cap reconstruction by widening a turn at some intermediate scale. The surviving difficulty is to derive a contradiction from the exact edge metric and the way the two annuli between the cap cycles embed in the common tube, including degenerate transverse polygons. We have not yet done that; none of the eight classes is proved impossible, and H13 at the hexagonal endpoint is still unresolved. Do not confuse the eight necessary limit families with eight solved cases.

The Type 4 tables were obtained by inspecting the face graph and adding the listed integer shifts by hand. The two-turn argument is related to the earlier Type 5 elongated-limit note, but the repeated-scale bounded-cap theorem and its application to both Type 4 markings are the new deductions here. No computer-assisted search or verification is a premise of the new geometric compactness arguments. The earlier geometric proof-audit qualifications and the winding classification premise for the tables still apply.
