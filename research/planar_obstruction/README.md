# Exact planar-pattern obstruction at the hexagonal metric

**Status:** None of Types 4, 5, or 7 is closed as a universality question. This checkpoint rules out one entire planar folding pattern (even on its nearest-lift boundary), rather than another bounded sample of 3D embeddings. It does not rule out the other planar patterns or an intrinsically flat embedded Type 5 torus that is not obtained from a planar inflation. Earlier marking and geometric proof-audit qualifications remain.

## 1. Clique-adjacent faces force a fold or a shortest-lift tie

Consider a piecewise affine map of a fixed flat triangulated torus into the plane, isometric on every nondegenerate face. It is globally 1-Lipschitz for intrinsic distances, by taking infima of path lengths. Every triangulation edge therefore realizes a shortest intrinsic geodesic (ties allowed). Define its face sign to be the orientation of its planar image relative to its intrinsic orientation. Two adjacent faces of equal sign agree with a single Euclidean isometry on their unfolded pair.

Call an edge ab clique-adjacent if its opposite face vertices u,v are also adjacent. Let K be the integral edge cocycle. If the two incident faces have equal signs, the edge uv and the unfolded vector from u via a to v both realize the same extrinsic length. If K_ua+K_av differs from K_uv, there are two distinct shortest lifts of uv. Thus if every edge has a unique shortest lift, every such dual adjacency must have opposite face signs. (The proof does not assume the planar map is injective.)

Exact enumeration over the 2^15 face signs modulo global reversal gives:

| Type | Clique-adjacent edges | Sign patterns reversing at all of them |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3 | 17 | 4 |
| 4 | 17 | 0 |
| 5 | 15 | 1 |
| 6 | 16 | 2 |
| 7 | 16 | 2 |

In particular the clique-adjacent dual graph of Type 4 is not bipartite. On its two sharp-ray markings all the relevant alternate shifts are distinct, so a planar construction must have a nearest-lift tie. For Type 5 all 15 alternate shifts are also distinct in the hexagonal representative. The remaining marking coverage follows the earlier marking classification, not a new proof of that classification.

## 2. The unique Type 5 all-clique-flip planar pattern is impossible at the hexagonal metric

This theorem allows weak shortest lifts; only nondegenerate planar faces and the indicated sign pattern are required. Use the Type 5 face list and marking `t5_ray_1.smt2` from the earlier checkpoint. The unique sign pattern, in its oriented face order, up to sign reversal, is `-++--+--+-+---++`. Its only three noncrease edges are 05, 16, 27. Each has opposite face vertices 3 and 4. All six triangles in these three unfolded quadrilaterals have the same sign. The anchor vertices are B=(5,1,2) and the associated leaves are L=(0,6,7). Write indices modulo three.

Normalize the planar positions of roots 3,4 to 0,1 in the complex plane. Their positions are distinct: the three unfolded root-to-root displacements differ by the lattice generators and have equal lengths. In fact their lifts have shifts (0,0),(-1,0),(0,-1). At the hexagonal metric these three displacements are the vectors from the vertices of an equilateral triangle to its circumcenter. After rescaling, rotating the intrinsic chart and reflecting the planar map if needed, they are R_i=omega^i, where omega=(-1+i sqrt(3))/2. The normalized lattice generators are h=1-omega and k=1-omega^2. Each unfolded quadrilateral is isometric with the same orientation: for its two vertices of group i, the intrinsic vector from root 3 is R_i Z, and from root 4 is R_i(Z-1), where Z is the planar image. The shifts and orientations are checked exactly in `verify.py`.

Let H be the Voronoi hexagon of this scaled lattice. Rotation by omega preserves H. Every group vertex is adjacent to both roots, so shortest-edge constraints give Z in H intersect (1+H). This intersection is the rhombus with corners 0,1,c,conjugate(c), where c=(1-i sqrt(3))/2. In particular Z-c belongs to the closed cone generated by u=-c and v=1-c.

Let A_i denote the planar position of anchor B_i. For the three anchor edges, the exact correction of the root-3-chart displacement is (R_i-R_j)c. Hence equality of their lengths is equivalent to

    |R_j (A_j-c) - R_i (A_i-c)|^2 = |A_j-A_i|^2.

For j=i+1 this gives

    <A_i-c, (omega-1)(A_j-c)> = 0,

where the inner product is the real Euclidean one. On the cone basis u,v its bilinear coefficient matrix is exactly

    [ -3/2    0   ]
    [ -3/2   -3/2 ].

Thus if both offsets are nonzero, the first is a positive multiple of u and the second a positive multiple of v. The cyclic three relations cannot hold with all three offsets nonzero. The anchors form an actual face (152), so two anchors cannot coincide at c. Exactly one, say A_i, equals c. The other two must lie respectively on the rays from c to 0 and from c to 1. Write A_(i+2)=(1-t)*1+t*c with 0<=t<1.

Now consider the leaf L_(i+1), with planar image Z. It is adjacent to A_i and A_(i+2). The cocycle correction for the first edge is the difference of the group rotations about root 4; for the second it is zero in the root 3 chart. Their length equations are therefore

    <Z-1, (omega^2-1)(c-1)> = 0,
    <Z, (omega-1) A_(i+2)> = 0.

The first coefficient vector is i sqrt(3), so Z is real. The real part of the second coefficient is -3(1-t)/2, strictly nonzero. Consequently Z=0. The leaf coincides with root 3 in the planar image, contradicting nondegeneracy of an incident triangular face.

This contradiction excludes the unique all-clique-flip pattern even when ties in the shortest-lift inequalities are permitted. In particular, any Type 5 planar inflation base at the hexagonal metric in the classified marking must have some equal-sign clique adjacency and a tie on its opposite edge. The theorem is not a nonuniversality theorem for Type 5: projection of a general 3D isometric embedding is not itself isometric face by face.

## 3. Computational exploration and lessons

- A new metric-circuit inequality using consistent cross-part intrinsic lifts was tested. Every resulting circuit exclusion was already present in the prior orientation formula for the tested remaining markings.
- Full disjoint-cycle linking constraints and convex-hull separation tests based on elongated limit partitions did not improve the projected clique-sign counts of the tested Type 4 and Type 5 markings. The Type 7 extensions were interrupted before completing the enumerations. Do not infer any new closure from these tests.
- The unique Type 5 all-clique-flip pattern was initially searched using its root charts and six anchor coordinates (300 numerical starts; no strict candidate). Section 2 replaces that numerical failure with an exact contradiction.
- In the Type 5 hexagonal representative, enumerating planar face signs, excluding a monochromatic vertex star (rank-seven regularity), and requiring consistent lifted coordinates for every monochromatic face component and mutually nearest shifts reduced 32,768 masks to 2,750 masks (2,724 distinct linear systems). Floating LP feasibility gave 2,522 masks (2,501 systems). This LP count is exploratory, not an exact infeasibility certificate. All 15 masks with exactly one noncrease clique adjacency passed that linear relaxation; a numerical search (120 starts, eight per mask) found no strict positive face-area candidate. That failure is not a proof. A partial nine-point orientation-extension SAT search also left many candidates.

The monochromatic-component formulation is a useful way of designing a systematic planar search: every vertex in a monochromatic component must have a unique intrinsic lift, all pairwise differences of its lifted vertices must be shortest lifts, and shared vertices constrain the component isometries. However, even a complete exclusion of all planar starting configurations would leave the non-planar 3D feasibility problem. Do not conflate closure of a construction class with classification of universality.

## 4. Reproduce

From this directory in the repository, run:

```
python3 verify.py ../linking_checkpoint/cases.json ../linking_checkpoint/lutz_faces.json
```

The verifier uses only the standard library, exhaustive finite sign enumeration, and exact rational arithmetic in Q(sqrt(-3)). `verification.txt` records its output. The geometric implications are proved above, not inferred from a numerical search. No independent proof audit is claimed.
